Mechanisms & Markers of Psychopathology

The biological and behavioral mechanisms and markers of mental illness — formerly taught as “Broken Brains.”

About the Course

What goes wrong in mental illness — and how we can tell.

This course integrates clinical psychology and cognitive neuroscience to explore the biological underpinnings of mental illness. It adopts a systems-level approach, examining the relationship between the function and dysfunction of specific brain circuits and networks and their contribution to mental health disorders. The brain’s reward system, for instance, has been linked to diagnoses such as addiction, disordered eating, depression, anxiety, posttraumatic stress disorder, and psychosis. Understanding this common neural foundation provides insights into how these disorders are interconnected and how this knowledge can advance treatment options.

The course draws on both traditional and cutting-edge methodologies — including large-scale neuroimaging efforts like the Human Connectome Project — and discusses how these findings inform treatment and complement societal conversations around issues such as racial bias and criminal responsibility.

Resources for Patients Awaiting Treatment

Students create resources aimed at individuals suffering from specific psychiatric disorders, to support them as they await treatment — patient psychoeducation materials with an audience beyond the classroom.
