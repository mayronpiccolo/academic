Being Alive: The Science and Experience of Being Human

In development · proposed Summer 2027

Being human as something to be studied — and something to be lived.

About the Course

Empirical research paired, week by week, with first-hand experience.

What is it actually like to be alive, and what does science know about it? This course treats being human as something to be studied and something to be lived. Each week pairs empirical research and a book chapter with a first-hand experience: singing together, moving hard, looking slowly at art, eating with attention, seeking awe, connecting with a stranger, and savoring pleasure.

We read to sharpen our attention, we go out to gather the raw material, and we return to think together about the gap between the paper and the lived thing. The course is deliberately experiential: most weeks include an activity at a site on or near campus — the Harvard Art Museums, the Arnold Arboretum, the Charles River — each with a low-cost, mobility-accessible alternative. The emphasis is on attention and reflection, not on athletic, musical, or artistic skill.
